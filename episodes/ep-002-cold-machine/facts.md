## 1. Persian yakhchāl

| ID | Safest true wording | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | A yakhchāl ("ice pit") stored ice made or gathered in winter so it could be sold in summer. In Iran it stored ice; it was not a cold store for food. | solid | Jørgensen, "Yaḵčāl", *Encyclopaedia Iranica* (2012, upd. 2017), https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/yakcal |
| F2 | Ice was made in shallow open-air basins. Then it was cut into blocks and carried into the pit. | solid | Iranica (as F1) |
| F3 | Tall shading walls stood on the south side of the basins, often also on the east and west, so the basin stayed in shadow in the low winter sun. Water came from qanāts (underground channels) and reservoirs. | solid | Iranica (as F1) |
| F4 | The storage building was a deep pit under a mud-brick dome, built from earth dug out on site, with mortar and plaster. Meltwater drained out through the bottom of the pit. | solid | Iranica (as F1) |
| F5 | "Sārooj" mortar of sand, clay, egg white, lime, goat hair and ash used in yakhchāls: repeated online (Wikipedia, from a 2012 source), but no analysis of actual yakhchāl mortar was found. ## 8. The vapour-compression cycle

| ID | Safest true wording | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| F35 | Four parts in a loop. (1) Evaporator, inside the cabinet: low-pressure liquid refrigerant boils, taking heat out of the food space; it leaves as cold, low-pressure vapour. (2) Compressor: squeezes the vapour to high pressure, which makes it hot (hotter than the kitchen). (3) Condenser, the coils at the back or under the fridge: the hot vapour gives its heat to the room air and condenses to a liquid, still at high pressure. (4) Expansion device (capillary tube in most home fridges, or a valve): the pressure drops sharply and the liquid's boiling point and temperature drop with it, ready to boil again in the evaporator. | solid | Penn State EGEE 102, https://courses.ems.psu.edu/egee102/node/2402 ; IIR (as F28) |

## Red flags (repeated online, false or overstated)

1. **"Yakhchāls are 2,400 years old / 400 BC."** An estimate. The surviving domes are mostly Safavid or Qajar (16th–19th c.). (F6–F8)
2. **"Sārooj = sand, clay, egg whites, lime, goat hair and ash" in ice houses.** No analysis of yakhchāl mortar supports it; Iranica says mud brick. (F5)
3. **"Yakhchāls kept food cold."** In Iran they stored ice to sell; food storage was the European ice-house use. (F1)
4. **Meybod "15 m" dome.** The scholarly figure is 18 m. (F8–F9)
5. **Barker's letter "describes Benares and Hooghly."** It names Allahabad, Mootegil and Calcutta. (F12)
6. **"Cullen's 1756 public demonstration in Edinburgh."** The paper is dated 1 May 1755, written as Glasgow professor, printed 1756; it describes laboratory trials, not a public show. (F22, F26)
7. **"Cullen froze water with diethyl ether."** The trial that froze water used "nitrous aether" (spirit of nitrous ether); he also tested the other ether. Say "ether". IIR's "evaporating water under a bell jar" is also loose. (F23)
8. **"Perkins' 1834 machine sold / launched refrigeration."** Never commercial. (F33)
9. **The Perkins "again and again … without waste" quote.** Attribution unverified (Perkins vs Evans). (F34)
10. **"Cleveland Clinic 1929: refrigerant leak killed 100+."** It was nitrocellulose X-ray film; Invention & Technology and others wrongly say methyl chloride. (F41)
11. **"Einstein read about a Berlin family killed by their fridge."** Only Szilárd's recollection, via Dannen 1997; no newspaper found. (F44)
12. **"Einstein–Szilárd patent applied for in 1926."** 1926 is the German priority date; the US application was filed 16 Dec 1927. (F43)
