# Facts, round 2: nutmeg as the main character (ep-001 additions)

Additions to `facts.md`, which stays valid. Answers the channel owner's round-2 review notes. Researched 2026-11-17.
Same confidence key as `facts.md`: **solid** / **contested** / **popular-myth**. Row numbers continue as R1, R2...
so they never clash with `facts.md`.

**New sources used this round (quality notes)**
- **Spurway's narrative (1617)**, printed in Kerr's *General History and Collection of Voyages* vol. 9, ch. 11, sect. 9:
  a contemporary English eyewitness account of Run, Dec 1616 to mid-1617
  ([franpritchett.com mirror](https://franpritchett.com/00generallinks/kerr/vol09chap11sect09.html)). Partisan (it was
  written as a complaint against the Dutch), but dates and ship details are first-hand.
- **Calendar of State Papers, East Indies (CSP EI)**, vols 3–4, on British History Online: summaries of the EIC's
  letters and depositions. Primary, but calendared (summarised) by a 19th-century editor.
- **Vincent C. Loth, "Armed Incidents and Unpaid Bills", *Modern Asian Studies* 29:4 (1995) 705–740**
  ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-asian-studies/article/armed-incidents-and-unpaid-bills-anglodutch-rivalry-in-the-banda-islands-in-the-seventeenth-century/D760CEE358E2FB1629EEBC2A1D9476D7)).
  The standard scholarly article on Anglo-Dutch Banda; only the abstract and notes could be read (paywall).
- **Pim de Zwart's VOC price dataset** (Dutch vs. Asian prices 1608–1800) and **Robert C. Allen's European wage
  dataset**, both hosted by the Global Price and Income History Group, UC Davis
  ([file list](https://gpih.ucdavis.edu/Datafilelist.htm)). Academic data, the best price source found.
- **Dorit Brixius**, *Journal of the Western Society for French History* 43 (2015), republished on
  [Brewminate](https://brewminate.com/pierre-poivre-the-french-spice-quest-and-the-role-of-mediators-in-southeast-asia-1740s-to-1770s/)
  (CC BY-NC-ND). Scholarly.
- Wikipedia articles are used where they cite scholarship; the scholar is named.

## R1. Toxicity: "harmless in the amounts we cook with"

| # | Claim | Figure | Source | Confidence | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1.1 | Dose that causes symptoms | About **5 g** of ground nutmeg at the lowest; poison-centre experience puts typical intoxication at **15–20 g, two to three tablespoons** | [Wikipedia: Myristicin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myristicin) (Rahman et al. 2015); [Vanderbilt Poison Center, 2020](https://www.vumc.org/poison-control/toxicology-question-week/nov-23-2020-nutmeg-potentially-uninvited-holiday-guest) (citing Ehrenpreis et al., *J Med Toxicol* 2014) | solid | A recipe uses a pinch to ¼ teaspoon (well under 1 g) per serving. |
| R1.2 | What it does | Starts 3–8 hours after eating; lasts "hours to days"; nausea, racing heart, dizziness, confusion, sometimes hallucinations; resembles anticholinergic poisoning | same | solid | The active compound is **myristicin** (also elemicin). No antidote; treatment is supportive. |
| R1.3 | Deaths | "Deaths have been reported, although this is extremely rare." Only **two** fatal cases in the literature: one over a century ago, one in 2001 that also involved a fatal dose of flunitrazepam | Vanderbilt (above); Ehrenpreis 2014 abstract via [PMC4057546](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4057546); [Cureus case report, 2024](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/case_report/pdf/327726/20241224-513806-7gbuve.pdf) | solid | A California review of 119 single-substance exposures (1997–2008) found no deaths ([Clin Toxicol 2011](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.3109/15563650.2011.561210)). |
| R1.4 | Food amounts | "Normal levels of intake of myristicin from spices in food is unlikely to cause these effects" | Wikipedia: Myristicin (Hallström & Thuvander 1997) | solid | **Safe script line:** "In the pinch you grate on eggnog it's harmless. Swallow two or three tablespoons and you'll spend a very bad day, and maybe the next one, being sick." Avoid "deadly". |

## Round-2 story beats (most dramatic sourced details)

1. **The goat and the tree.** On 14 Dec 1616 Run's chiefs handed the English a nutmeg tree, a clod of earth, fruit and a live goat as a "symbolical surrender", and the *Swan* fired 30 guns (Spurway, R3.5).
2. **"Give us the island and you can have your ship back."** Reael's offer, refused by Courthope in April 1617 (R5.6).
3. **Mutiny.** While Courthope's men were building a gun battery on Nailaka, nine of them cut the *Defence*'s cables and sailed her to the enemy (R5.5). From then on the English had no ship at all.
4. **"Saint George was now turned a child."** The Dutch taunt after taking the *Swan* in a 90-minute fight (R5.4).
5. **A canoe with 40 Bandanese** carried Muschamp's plea for help across the sea in Aug 1618 (R5.9). The help sent, the *Solomon* and *Attendant*, was captured (R5.8).
6. **London sent a pay rise, not a warship** (£100 a year, Jan 1620), while the Company made peace in Europe (R5.12–R5.14).
7. **He died coming back from accepting another island.** Courthope had gone to Lonthor to receive its surrender; the Dutch caught him on the way home; shot in the chest, he went over the side and was never seen again (R6.1–R6.3).
8. **Cutting the trees to make a claim worthless** (Burnett, R7.1), then inspecting the empty island every year for 45 years (R7.2).
9. **Coen's face.** A contemporary portrait survives (Waben, Westfries Museum); Courthope's does not (R8.1, R8.3). That asymmetry is itself the story: the winners kept the portraits.
10. **The price.** In 1626 a kilo of nutmeg cost an Amsterdam labourer about a week's pay; the same 60 guilders that "bought" Manhattan would have bought about 14 kg of nutmeg (R9.3, R12.3).
11. **Ten seconds.** Run's whole nutmeg crop today equals about ten seconds of Manhattan's economy (R14.4).
12. **The language in exile.** Bandanese survives only in two villages on Kei Besar, Banda Eli and Banda Elat, founded by people who escaped in 1621 (R13.3).
13. **Pepper's revenge.** The man who broke the monopoly was named *Poivre*, "pepper", and had lost an arm to a cannonball (R11.5).
