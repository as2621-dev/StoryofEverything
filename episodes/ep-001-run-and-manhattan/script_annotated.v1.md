## 1a Two islands, one treaty

[VISUAL: Navy night theme. A flat paper-cutout world map. A date stamp thumps down in the corner: 31 JULY 1667 · BREDA. Two cutout envoys, one English, one Dutch, sign a scroll over the North Sea.]

On the thirty-first of July, sixteen sixty-seven, in the Dutch town of Breda, English and Dutch envoys signed a peace. Look at the two islands it settled.

[VISUAL @ "On the left,": Amber pin on the mouth of the Hudson. Cutout Manhattan outline lifts off the map, label MANHATTAN.]

On the left, Manhattan. A long island at the mouth of a great river.

[VISUAL @ "On the right,": A dotted line runs across the globe to the Banda Sea, counter "≈ 15,350 KM". Zoom past the Banda islands to a speck at the western edge. Label: RUN.]

On the right, fifteen thousand kilometers away, an island most maps leave off. This is Run.

[VISUAL @ "Three kilometers long.": Run cutout fills the frame with a scale bar "3 KM × 1 KM". A green Central Park outline slides over it; the two shapes nearly match. Card: "ABOUT THE SIZE OF CENTRAL PARK".]

Three kilometers long. One wide. About the size of Central Park.

[VISUAL @ "And the story goes": Manhattan outline drops in beside Run; Run-shaped tiles fill it, more than a dozen of them. Then the Manhattan cutout slides away and Run stays, alone, under a single amber pin.]

And the story goes that at Breda, the Dutch had to pick one. They chose the speck.

## 1b Not a swap

[VISUAL: Cream theme. Headline card in serif, one italic red word: "Why *trade* Manhattan for this?" A walking-figure icon crosses Run with a timer: "≈ 40 MIN".]

So, why did the Dutch trade Manhattan for a tiny island you can walk across in forty minutes?

[VISUAL @ "Here's the first problem.": A treaty-scroll cutout unrolls. A red stamp lands on two words: UTI POSSIDETIS. Subtitle under it: "WHAT YOU HAVE, YOU HOLD".]

Here's the first problem. They didn't. The treaty ran on an old Latin rule, uti possidetis. What you have, you hold. Each side kept whatever it held on the twentieth of May, sixteen sixty-seven.

[VISUAL @ "England had seized": Split map. Manhattan pin turns red, tag "ENGLISH SINCE 1664". Run pin turns orange, tag "DUTCH SINCE 1621". Between them, an arrow labelled "SWAP" is struck through in red.]

England had seized Manhattan three years earlier. The Dutch had held Run for more than forty. Nobody handed an island over. Each side just gave up its claim to the other's.

[VISUAL @ "Which leaves": Camera pushes in on Run until it fills the screen. A cutout question mark settles over it.]

[VISUAL @ "And why did the Dutch,": The camera pulls back to the two-island map; Manhattan glows, then dims as Run brightens. Small tag: "THE DUTCH WERE WINNING".]

Which leaves the real question. Why was a three-kilometer island worth fighting over at all? And why did the Dutch, who were winning that war, let Manhattan go and keep the speck?

[VISUAL @ "The answer comes": Five numbered cutout markers pop along a dotted line: a nutmeg fruit, a route line, a company seal, a fort, a treaty scroll. The fifth marker shows a tiny flag, greyed out.]

The answer comes in five steps. A seed. A route. A company. A siege. And a treaty. The last one ends on a national flag, on the other side of the world.

## 2a A seed

[VISUAL: Cream. A cutout map of the Banda Islands, west to east: Run, Ai, Gunung Api, Banda Neira, the long arc of Lonthor. A scale bar spans them: "ABOUT 20 KM". Marker 1 lights up.]

[VISUAL @ "Four hours on foot,": A walking-figure icon strides along a dotted line drawn across the water from Run to Lonthor; timer "≈ 4 H".]

Step one, a seed. These are the Banda Islands, a handful of volcanic specks in eastern Indonesia. End to end, about twenty kilometers. Four hours on foot, if the sea were a road.

[VISUAL @ "Here grows": A cutout nutmeg fruit hangs on a branch, then splits open. Inside: a brown seed wrapped in a red lace net. Labels draw on: NUTMEG (the seed), MACE (the red net).]

Here grows a tree. Split its fruit open. Inside, a brown seed wrapped in a red, lacy net. The seed is nutmeg. The net is mace. One fruit. Two spices.

[VISUAL @ "And for centuries,": World map, cream. Every coastline fades to grey except the Banda group, which glows amber. Card: "THE WORLD'S ONLY SOURCE".]

And for centuries, every nutmeg in the world came from these few islands.

[VISUAL @ "Keep one number": A sapling cutout grows beside a year counter: YEAR 1… YEAR 7–9 "FIRST FRUIT"… YEAR 20 "FULL CROP". A small pin tag: "REMEMBER THIS".]

Keep one number in mind. A new nutmeg tree gives its first fruit after seven to nine years. You can't grow nutmeg in a hurry.

## 2b A route

[VISUAL: Map zooms out to the Indian Ocean. Marker 2 lights up. A red route draws west from Banda; small sail cutouts ride it. Pin: MALACCA. Counter: "> 3,000 KM".]

Step two, a route. For thousands of years, nutmeg left the slow way. Bandanese, Javanese and Malay boats carried it more than three thousand kilometers west, to Malacca.

[VISUAL @ "From Malacca,": The line keeps drawing, pins dropping in rhythm: CALICUT. Then it forks: one branch up the Red Sea toward ALEXANDRIA, one up the Gulf toward the Syrian coast. Both meet a last leg by sea: VENICE.]

From Malacca, to India. Across the Arabian Sea. Up the Red Sea toward Alexandria, or up the Gulf toward Syria. Then by ship, to Venice.

[VISUAL @ "In a straight line,": A distance counter ticks to "≈ 14,400 KM". A thin globe ring shows the share of Earth's circumference, just over a third. Small mono label: "STRAIGHT LINE · REAL ROUTES WERE LONGER".]

In a straight line, that's about fourteen thousand kilometers. More than a third of the way around the planet.

[VISUAL @ "And at every port,": Merchant silhouettes stand at each pin. A price tag on a single nutmeg grows a little larger at each stop. Mono tag: "CHANGED HANDS AGAIN AND AGAIN".]

And at every port, it changed hands. Each buyer paid the last man's price and added his own.

[VISUAL @ "By one historian's": A price card: "BANDA ×1 → AMSTERDAM ×122". Source tag: "AMY JORDAN, 2016". Beside it a cutout coffee cup: "$2" swells to "$200+".]

By one historian's estimate, nutmeg came to sell in Amsterdam for over a hundred times what it cost in Banda. That's a two-dollar coffee selling for more than two hundred.

## 3a A company

[VISUAL: Marker 3 lights up. A Portuguese ship cutout sails from Malacca, stamp 1511, to Banda, stamp 1512. On Banda, a fort outline tries to draw itself and is rubbed out.]

Step three, a company. Whoever could sail straight to Banda, past every middleman, would get rich. The Portuguese tried first, in fifteen twelve. But the Bandanese never let them build a lasting fort.

[VISUAL @ "Then came the Dutch.": Dutch East Indiaman cutouts arrive, stamp 1599. A card slides in: "DUTCH EAST INDIA COMPANY · 20 MARCH 1602". Four icons draw on under it: crossed swords, a treaty seal, a coin, a fort.]

[VISUAL @ "The VOC could": The four power icons stamp down one by one as each is named.]

Then came the Dutch, in fifteen ninety-nine. On the twentieth of March, sixteen-oh-two, they founded the Dutch East India Company. The VOC could wage war, sign treaties, mint coins and build forts. A business with its own army.

[VISUAL @ "And its plan": The whole relay route from 2b lights up again. One by one the merchant silhouettes along it are struck through in red, until one orange line runs unbroken from Banda to Amsterdam.]

And its plan was not to join the relay. It was to own it. If every nutmeg on Earth grew here, whoever held these islands set the price. No rivals. No leaks. Every tree.

[VISUAL @ "And at the western edge": Slow pan west across the Banda map to Run. A single English flag cutout flickers on the horizon.]

And at the western edge of the map, one small island was about to let in a rival.

## 4a Thirty-nine men

[VISUAL: Marker 4 lights up. Run cutout, close. Two English ship cutouts labelled SWAN and DEFENCE sail in from the west, from a pin marked BANTAM. Stamp: DECEMBER 1616.]

Step four, a siege. In December sixteen sixteen, two English ships, the Swan and the Defence, anchored off Run. In command was Nathaniel Courthope, of the English East India Company.

[VISUAL @ "Earlier that year,": Ai, the next island east, flips orange. Stamp: APRIL 1616. Tiny boat cutouts cross the strait toward Run; a counter beside them reads "≈ 400 DROWNED".]

[VISUAL @ "So when the English": Back on Run, island silhouettes meet the English landing party on the beach.]

Earlier that year, the Dutch had taken Ai, the next island east. About four hundred islanders drowned trying to flee to Run. So when the English arrived, Run's people made them an offer.

[VISUAL @ "An English letter": A cutout of a handwritten letter, 1616. One line is drawn over in red ink: "surrender their island of Puloroon to our sovereign the king of England". Small tag: "PULOROON = RUN".]

An English letter records it. The islanders agreed to "surrender their island of Puloroon to our sovereign the king of England." A king none of them had ever seen.

[VISUAL @ "To hold it,": A small fort cutout on Run. A waffle grid of 39 figures fills beside it. Across the strait, the VOC fleet icons from 3a stack up, many times larger.]

To hold it, Courthope had thirty-nine men. Against a company with its own navy.

## 4b The siege

[VISUAL: Three Dutch ship cutouts rise on the horizon. Stamp: 3 JANUARY 1617. A day counter starts in the corner: DAY 1.]

Dutch ships arrived within three weeks. The Swan was seized. The Defence was lost. Food ran short, and water too. By one account, the island has no springs.

[VISUAL @ "Courthope's plan": A cutout ship's-letter folds and sails west toward a London pin. The day counter climbs: DAY 365… DAY 730… DAY 1,095.]

Courthope's plan was simple. Hold the island until London sent help. Day one became a year. Then two. Then three.

[VISUAL @ "But in January": Cream, London. A ledger-page cutout: "£100 A YEAR · RECOMMENDED FOR PROMOTION". Stamp: JANUARY 1620. Counter: DAY ≈ 1,100. The light warms.]

But in January sixteen twenty, the Company's directors in London voted him a reward. A hundred pounds a year, and a promotion. London had not forgotten him.

[VISUAL @ "He never heard": Navy. A small boat cutout off the long arc of Lonthor, Dutch ship silhouettes ahead. Stamp: c. 20 OCTOBER 1620. Quote card, red underline: "received a shot in the breast". Source tag: "DICTIONARY OF NATIONAL BIOGRAPHY, 1887".]

[VISUAL @ "A biography from": The boat cutout rocks; a single splash ring spreads on the water and fades. The quote card holds.]

He never heard about it. Around the twentieth of October, sixteen twenty, Courthope went out by boat after Dutch ships. A biography from eighteen eighty-seven says he "received a shot in the breast," leapt overboard, and was never seen again.

[PAUSE 2s]

[VISUAL @ "He had held": The day counter stops at "≈ 1,400 DAYS". The 39-figure waffle fades. The English flag on Run lowers.]

He had held Run for nearly four years. Soon after, the English left.

[VISUAL @ "Within a week,": A second letter cutout slides in, dated 26 OCTOBER 1620, sealed in orange wax.]

Within a week, another man wrote a letter.

## 5a Sixteen twenty-one

[VISUAL: Navy. A cutout silhouette of a man in a ruff, label "JAN PIETERSZOON COEN · VOC GOVERNOR-GENERAL". The letter from 4b opens; one line is drawn over in red: "to once again subjugate Banda, and populate it with other people".]

[VISUAL @ "His motto was": A second quote card slides under the first: "Despair not, spare not your enemies" · 1618.]

He was Jan Pieterszoon Coen, the VOC's governor-general in Asia. It was necessary, he wrote, "to once again subjugate Banda, and populate it with other people." His motto was "Despair not, spare not your enemies."

[VISUAL @ "He sailed": A route draws from BATAVIA to Banda. Waffle counts appear along it: 19 ship icons, then soldier icons: "1,655 DUTCH SOLDIERS", "286 JAPANESE MERCENARIES". Beside it, the 39-figure waffle from 4a, dwarfed: "≈ 50 : 1". Stamp: 12 MARCH 1621 on Lonthor.]

He sailed from Batavia with nineteen ships, sixteen hundred and fifty-five Dutch soldiers, and two hundred and eighty-six Japanese mercenaries. Fifty men for every one Courthope had. By the twelfth of March, sixteen twenty-one, the main island was his.

[VISUAL @ "In April,": Stamp: 21 APRIL 1621, then 8 MAY 1621. A row of chief silhouettes goes dark. A column of small figures, counter "789+", moves off the map toward Batavia.]

In April, confessions to a plot were taken under torture. In May, dozens of the island chiefs, the orang kaya, were beheaded by the Japanese mercenaries.

[VISUAL @ "Before the conquest,": A waffle of 15 figures, each standing for about a thousand people. Fourteen fade to empty outlines, one at a time. One remains. Mono label: "≈ 15,000 → ≈ 1,000 · ESTIMATES".]

Before the conquest, an estimated fifteen thousand people lived on these islands. Afterward, about one thousand. Of every fifteen people, one remained.

[PAUSE 2s]

[VISUAL @ "The nutmeg land": The Banda map is cut by thin lines into 68 plots. Arrows bring silhouettes in from the Indies, India and China. Card: "68 PLANTATIONS".]

The nutmeg land was cut into sixty-eight plantations, worked by enslaved people brought from across the Indies, India and China.

## 5b Every tree on Run

[VISUAL: Navy. Run alone on the map. Small nutmeg-tree cutouts dot the island.]

Run came last. The Dutch killed or enslaved the men. They sent the women and children away.

[VISUAL @ "And they cut down": The tree cutouts tip over and vanish, one after another, until the island is bare.]

And they cut down every nutmeg tree on the island.

[PAUSE 2s]

[VISUAL @ "Remember the clock": The sapling counter from 2a returns: "7–9 YEARS TO FIRST FRUIT". Then cattle silhouettes wander across the bare island.]

Remember the clock on a nutmeg tree. Seven to nine years. Whatever England claimed now, there was nothing on Run to take.

[VISUAL @ "Meanwhile, the Dutch": Map tilts west, toward the Atlantic, with a red dotted line pointing to the Hudson.]

Meanwhile, the Dutch were about to build on another island.

## 6a The other island

[VISUAL: Cream. The globe swings about 15,000 km west from Run to Manhattan. A small Dutch fort cutout appears on the island's southern tip. Stamp: MID-1620s.]

[VISUAL @ "A letter dated": Cutout letter, Amsterdam, dated 5 NOVEMBER 1626. One line underlined in red: "bought Manhattes Island from the Native People for 60 guilders worth of trade". Mono tag: "PIETER SCHAGHEN'S LETTER · THE ONLY RECORD".]

Now look at Manhattan. In the mid-sixteen twenties, the Dutch founded a colony there. A letter dated the fifth of November, sixteen twenty-six, says they "bought Manhattes Island from the Native People for 60 guilders worth of trade."

[VISUAL @ "The Lenape": Two silhouettes, one Lenape, one Dutch, each holding a different-shaped card: "SALE" and "SHARED USE?". Mono tag: "NO DEED SURVIVES".]

The Lenape did not share the European idea of selling land.

[VISUAL @ "The ship carrying": A ship cutout. Its hold fills with beaver-pelt icons as a counter runs to "7,246". Beside it, a scale: "60 GUILDERS" against "≈ 1,000×".]

The ship carrying that news also carried seven thousand two hundred and forty-six beaver pelts. By one estimate, worth about a thousand times the island.

[VISUAL @ "In sixteen sixty-four,": English squadron cutouts enter the harbor. Stamp: 1664 · PEACETIME. The label NEW AMSTERDAM flips to NEW YORK.]

In sixteen sixty-four, an English squadron sailed into the harbor, and the Dutch governor surrendered. War had not even been declared. The town became New York. Three years later, a treaty would decide who kept it.

## 6b Breda

[VISUAL: Navy. Marker 5 lights up. A cutout map of the Medway estuary in England. English ship silhouettes catch fire in red. Stamp: JUNE 1667.]

Step five, a treaty. But first, a war. In June sixteen sixty-seven, Dutch ships sailed into the Medway, in England, and burned the English fleet. Six weeks later came Breda.

[VISUAL @ "So the Dutch": The treaty scroll from 1b returns, in two columns. ENGLAND KEEPS: NEW NETHERLAND. THE DUTCH KEEP: RUN, SURINAME.]

So the Dutch held the stronger hand. Under "what you have, you hold," they kept Run, and Suriname, then the bigger prize. England kept New Netherland.

[VISUAL @ "Since sixteen sixteen,": Run alone. A red paper strip labelled "ENGLISH CLAIM · 1616" lies across it; under it, a second strip: "TREATY OF 1654: RETURN RUN TO ENGLAND".]

Since sixteen sixteen, England had claimed Run. A treaty in sixteen fifty-four had even said Run should go back to England.

[VISUAL @ "To the Dutch,": The relay route lights up orange from Banda to Amsterdam, one unbroken line. On Run, a small gap in the line glows red, then closes. Manhattan sits in the corner under a beaver-pelt icon.]

[VISUAL @ "At Breda, the claim": The red claim strips on Run tear and blow away. The orange line is whole.]

To the Dutch, Run was never just a small island. It was the last hole in the fence. While that claim stood, a rival could come back and replant the trees. At Breda, the claim was gone.

[PAUSE 2s]

[VISUAL @ "So, did the Dutch": Back to the two-island map from 1a, cream. Manhattan under a fur icon, Run under a nutmeg.]

So, did the Dutch choose the speck? Not in a swap. But they kept the island their monopoly needed, and let the fur post go.

## 7a The leak

[VISUAL: Cream. Map of the Indian Ocean. The orange Dutch line still runs unbroken. A crack appears at its western end.]

And yet, a monopoly on a living thing lasts only as long as every seed stays home.

[VISUAL @ "In the late": A cutout silhouette in a tricorn hat, label "PIERRE POIVRE · INTENDANT OF MAURITIUS". A small boat slips out of the Moluccas with potted seedlings; a dotted line runs to MAURITIUS. Stamp: EARLY 1770s.]

[VISUAL @ "By the early": Pamplemousses garden cutout near Port Louis, a small nutmeg sapling in a bed. The crack in the orange line widens.]

In the late seventeen-sixties, Pierre Poivre, the French official running Mauritius, sent secret expeditions to smuggle nutmeg plants out. By the early seventeen-seventies, they were growing on his island.

[VISUAL @ "But Britain": Fort cutout on Banda Neira. Stamps: 1796, then 9 AUGUST 1810 · DAWN. Seedling pots with soil load onto a ship.]

But Britain did the real damage. British forces took the Bandas in seventeen ninety-six, and again in eighteen ten, storming the fort on Banda Neira at dawn.

[VISUAL @ "They dug up": Red arrows fan out from Banda carrying seedling pots: PENANG, CEYLON, SINGAPORE. A long arrow continues across the Atlantic to GRENADA.]

They dug up seedlings, packed them with soil, and shipped them to Penang, to Ceylon, to Singapore. From there, nutmeg reached Grenada, in the Caribbean.

[VISUAL @ "Look at Grenada's flag.": Grenada flag cutout. The camera pushes into the left-hand triangle, where a small nutmeg sits. Grey marker 5's flag icon turns to full colour.]

Look at Grenada's flag. In the triangle on the left, there's a nutmeg.

## 7b Two islands, again

[VISUAL: Cream. A one-kilo paper bag cutout beside a bag of sugar of the same size. Price card: "INDONESIA, FARM GATE · ≈ $5–7 / KG". The coffee cup from 2b returns, three of them in a row.]

Today, a farmer in Indonesia gets about five to seven dollars for a kilo of nutmeg. A bag the weight of a bag of sugar. About three of those two-dollar coffees.

[VISUAL @ "The fight for": Navy. A ledger card lists three lines: "COURTHOPE · 1620", "BANDA · ≈ 9 IN 10 PEOPLE", "RUN · EVERY TREE".]

The fight for this speck cost Courthope his life, the Bandanese about nine in every ten of their people, and Run every tree it had.

[VISUAL @ "Back to the map.": The opening navy map from 1a. Two amber pins. Manhattan labelled NEW YORK. Run, small, with nutmeg-tree cutouts growing back; counter "POP. 1,572".]

Back to the map. One island became New York. The other is still three kilometers long, home to fifteen hundred and seventy-two people. And the nutmeg grew back.

[VISUAL @ "In the middle": Slow push into Manhattan. The green Central Park outline from 1a glows in the middle of it, the same shape that once sat over Run. Hold.]

In the middle of Manhattan, there's a park about the size of Run.
